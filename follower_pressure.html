5. Follower pressure#

A gas or a liquid presses normal to the current surface of the shell and on its current area: the pressure follows the deformation. A dead pressure, in contrast, keeps the direction of the undeformed normal and the undeformed area. The difference matters for the buckling of rings and cylinders under external pressure, for which the buckling pressure of a follower load and of a dead load differ, and for any nonlinear analysis with pressure. This chapter derives the force vector of a follower pressure, its load stiffness matrix and the conditions under which this matrix is symmetric. Two functions of each kinematics implement them, update_fext_follower() and update_KCfollower().

5.1. Virtual work of the pressure#

The pressure \(p(x, y)\) is given per unit area of the undeformed mid-surface, as a function of the undeformed coordinates, a body-attached follower load in the classification of Schweizerhof and Ramm [SchweizerhofRamm1984]. With \(\boldsymbol{X} = \boldsymbol{X}_0 + \boldsymbol{u}\) the position of the deformed mid-surface, the pressure does the virtual work

(5.1)#\[\delta W_p = \int_A p \, \boldsymbol{a}^\top \delta \boldsymbol{u} \, dA, \qquad \boldsymbol{a} = \boldsymbol{X}_{,x} \times \boldsymbol{X}_{,y}\]

where \(\boldsymbol{a}\) is the area vector of the deformed mid-surface per unit undeformed area and \(\delta \boldsymbol{u} = \{\delta u, \delta v, \delta w\}^\top\). With the turning basis of Eq. (1.2), the tangent vectors in the basis of the undeformed mid-surface are

(5.2)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{X}_{,x} = \begin{Bmatrix} 1 + u_{,x} \\ v_{,x} \\ w_{,x} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{X}_{,y} = \begin{Bmatrix} u_{,y} \\ 1 + v_{,y} + w/R \\ w_{,y} - k_v v \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

with \(k_v\) of Eq. (1.4): the Sanders-type kinematics, \(k_v = 1/R\), give the exact area vector, and the Donnell-type kinematics neglect the term \(v/R\) of the rotation of the normal, as their strains do. A positive \(p\) pushes along \(+\boldsymbol{e}_z\), outwards, the direction of a positive \(w\): an external pressure is negative.

5.2. Truncation of the area vector#

The area vector is split into its parts of order 0, 1 and 2 in the displacement gradients, \(\boldsymbol{a} = \boldsymbol{a}_0 + \boldsymbol{a}_1 + \boldsymbol{a}_2\):

(5.3)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{a}_0 = \begin{Bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{a}_1 = \begin{Bmatrix} -w_{,x} \\ -w_{,y} + k_v v \\ u_{,x} + v_{,y} + w/R \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{a}_2 = \begin{Bmatrix} v_{,x} (w_{,y} - k_v v) - w_{,x} (v_{,y} + w/R) \\ w_{,x} u_{,y} - u_{,x} (w_{,y} - k_v v) \\ u_{,x} (v_{,y} + w/R) - v_{,x} u_{,y} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

\(\boldsymbol{a}_0\) is the dead load; \(\boldsymbol{a}_1\) has the rotations of the normal, \(\beta_x\) and \(\beta_y\), in its in-plane components and the change of area, the linear membrane dilatation \(\varepsilon_{xx} + \varepsilon_{yy}\), in its normal component. With the ordering of the moderate rotations of the strains, \(u_{,x} \sim \epsilon\) and \(w_{,x} \sim \epsilon^{1/2}\), \(\boldsymbol{a}_0 + \boldsymbol{a}_1\) keeps every term of \(p \, \boldsymbol{a}^\top \delta \boldsymbol{u}\) up to the order \(\epsilon p \, \delta w\) of the strains, and \(\boldsymbol{a}_2\) adds terms of order \(\epsilon^2 p \, \delta w\), of the order of those that the strains neglect.

The functions take two flags. quadratic=0, the default, uses the consistent truncation \(\boldsymbol{a}_0 + \boldsymbol{a}_1\), and quadratic=1 adds \(\boldsymbol{a}_2\), the complete area vector of the kinematics, e.g. to check that a result does not depend on the truncation. reference=0 leaves out \(\boldsymbol{a}_0\). With the interpolation of The BFSC element on the cylinder, the force vector is

(5.4)#\[\boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{u}_e) = \int_A p \left( (\boldsymbol{S}^u)^\top a_x + (\boldsymbol{S}^v)^\top a_y + (\boldsymbol{S}^w)^\top a_z \right) dA, \qquad \boldsymbol{a} = r \boldsymbol{a}_0 + \boldsymbol{a}_1 + q \boldsymbol{a}_2\]

with \(r\) = reference and \(q\) = quadratic. At \(\boldsymbol{u}_e = \boldsymbol{0}\) it is the consistent load vector of a dead pressure, \(\int_A p (\boldsymbol{S}^w)^\top dA\), for both truncations; with reference=0 it is \(\boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{u}_e) - \boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{0})\), the part that depends on the displacements. For quadratic=0, \(\boldsymbol{F}_p\) is affine in the displacements.

The pressure is given to the functions at the integration points of each element, as an array p of shape (nint, nint), p[i, j] at \(\xi\) = points[i] and \(\eta\) = points[j], like the stiffnesses of Constitutive relation, multiplied by the load factor of the analysis. A pressure that depends on the current position, e.g. a hydrostatic head of a liquid, is not modelled.

5.3. Load stiffness matrix#

The load stiffness matrix of update_KCfollower() is the derivative of the force vector, with the sign of a stiffness:

(5.5)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_F = -\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{F}_p}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e} = -\int_A p \left( (\boldsymbol{S}^u)^\top \frac{\partial a_x}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e} + (\boldsymbol{S}^v)^\top \frac{\partial a_y}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e} + (\boldsymbol{S}^w)^\top \frac{\partial a_z}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e} \right) dA\]

with the derivatives of the displacement gradients, e.g. \(\partial u_{,x}/\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e = \boldsymbol{S}^u_{,x}\). \(\boldsymbol{a}_0\) does not contribute. For quadratic=0 the matrix is constant for a given pressure,

(5.6)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_F = \int_A p \left( (\boldsymbol{S}^u)^\top \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,x} + (\boldsymbol{S}^v)^\top \left( \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,y} - k_v \boldsymbol{S}^v \right) - (\boldsymbol{S}^w)^\top \left( \boldsymbol{S}^u_{,x} + \boldsymbol{S}^v_{,y} + \boldsymbol{S}^w/R \right) \right) dA\]

and for quadratic=1 it is linear in the displacements. The residual of a nonlinear analysis with the follower pressure and its Jacobian are

(5.7)#\[\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{u}) = \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u}) - \boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{u}), \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_T = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_G + \boldsymbol{K}_F\]

with the matrices of Eq. (4.4) and \(\boldsymbol{F}_p\) and \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\) of the same pressure and truncation.

5.4. Symmetry of the load stiffness matrix#

\(\boldsymbol{K}_F\) is unsymmetric in general: the follower pressure is a non-conservative load. For the first-order area vector, the skew part of the bilinear form \(P(\delta \boldsymbol{u}, \Delta \boldsymbol{u}) = \int_A p \, \delta \boldsymbol{u}^\top \boldsymbol{a}_1(\Delta \boldsymbol{u}) \, dA\) over the loaded region is, after an integration by parts,

(5.8)#\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} P(\delta \boldsymbol{u}, \Delta \boldsymbol{u}) - P(\Delta \boldsymbol{u}, \delta \boldsymbol{u}) = &-\oint p \left( (\delta u \Delta w - \delta w \Delta u) n_x + (\delta v \Delta w - \delta w \Delta v) n_y \right) ds \\ &+ \int_A \left( p_{,x} (\delta u \Delta w - \delta w \Delta u) + p_{,y} (\delta v \Delta w - \delta w \Delta v) \right) dA \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

with \(\{n_x, n_y\}^\top\) the outward normal of the contour of the loaded region. The terms of the curvature of the cylinder, \(w/R\) and \(k_v v\), are symmetric and cancel, such that Eq. (5.8) holds for both kinematics. The load stiffness matrix is therefore symmetric, and the follower pressure conservative, if and only if:

  • \(p\) is uniform over the loaded region, such that the area integral vanishes; and

  • at each point of the contour of the loaded region, either \(w = 0\) or the in-plane displacement normal to the contour vanishes, \(u = 0\) on an edge \(x\) = constant and \(v = 0\) on an edge \(y\) = constant, such that the contour integral vanishes.

Simply supported and clamped edges, symmetry planes and closed surfaces meet these conditions; for a cylinder closed around the circumference, the contour of the loaded region is that of its ends. The load potential is then the pressure times the change of the enclosed volume. Free or partially restrained edges, a pressure applied on a patch whose edges lie inside the shell, and a pressure that varies over the surface give an unsymmetric matrix. These are the results of Hibbitt [Hibbitt1979] and of Schweizerhof and Ramm [SchweizerhofRamm1984], who showed that the pressure load stiffness is symmetric for closed surfaces or loaded surfaces with a fixed boundary.

For the matrix of one element, the contour of the loaded region is the boundary of the element, where the second condition does not hold: the element matrix is unsymmetric. In the assembled matrix, the contour integrals along an edge shared by two elements cancel when \(p\) is continuous, since \(u\), \(v\) and \(w\) are continuous, and the conditions apply to the contour of the loaded region of the whole shell, restricted to the unknown degrees of freedom. The matrices are written in full and must not be symmetrized; the linear systems and eigenvalue problems that contain \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\) are solved with solvers of unsymmetric matrices. For quadratic=1 the matrix depends on the displacements and these conditions do not apply.

5.5. Linear buckling under a follower pressure#

For a pressure \(\lambda p_0\) proportional to the load multiplier, the tangent stiffness matrix of the prebuckling state \(\lambda \boldsymbol{u}_0\), linearised in \(\lambda\), adds the load stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}}\) of \(p_0\) to the geometric stiffness matrix of the linear static solution \(\boldsymbol{u}_0\) of \(p_0\):

(5.9)#\[\left( \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda \left( \boldsymbol{K}_G (\boldsymbol{u}_0) + \boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}} \right) \right) \boldsymbol{\phi} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

with quadratic=0, an eigenvalue problem that is unsymmetric in general, see Analyses. For a dead pressure, \(\boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}}\) is left out. The two loads give different buckling pressures: for a ring in generalised plane strain buckling with \(n = 2\) circumferential waves, the inextensional limit of the exact second variation of the ring with the strains of Kinematics of the cylindrical shell gives

With the Sanders-type kinematics and a dead pressure, a ring free to rotate has a spurious buckling mode at a vanishing load: the rigid rotation \(v\) = constant under the hoop prestress \(N_{yy} = pR\) has the energy \(\int_A N_{yy} (v/R)^2 dA\) in \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\), negative under an external pressure, which the follower load stiffness, \(-\int_A p v^2/R \, dA\), cancels.

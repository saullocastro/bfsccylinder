3. The BFSC element on the cylinder#

The element interpolates the three displacements of the mid-surface with products of cubic Hermite polynomials, whose nodal values are the displacements and their derivatives. For the normal displacement \(w\) this is the plate-bending rectangle of Bogner, Fox and Schmit [Bogner1965], the BFS element, with the cross derivative \(w_{,xy}\) as a degree of freedom; the in-plane displacements \(u\) and \(v\) use the same functions without the cross derivative, as in the BFSC plate element of Castro and Jansen [CastroJansen2021]. The single-curvature version, for the developed mid-surface of a cylinder, was presented by Castro and Jansen [CastroJansen2022] with Donnell-type and Sanders-type kinematics. The interpolation and the integration are the same for both kinematics.

3.1. Geometry and natural coordinates#

The element is a rectangle of the developed mid-surface, of sides \(l_{ex}\) = lex along \(x\) and \(l_{ey}\) = ley along \(y = R\theta\), with \(R\) = R. The natural coordinates \(\xi, \eta \in [-1, 1]\) give

(3.1)#\[x = x_1 + \frac{l_{ex}}{2} (\xi + 1), \qquad y = y_1 + \frac{l_{ey}}{2} (\eta + 1), \qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial x} = \frac{2}{l_{ex}} \frac{\partial}{\partial \xi}, \qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial y} = \frac{2}{l_{ey}} \frac{\partial}{\partial \eta}, \qquad dA = \frac{l_{ex} l_{ey}}{4} d\xi \, d\eta\]

with \((x_1, y_1)\) the position of node 1. The nodes are numbered counterclockwise from the corner of the smallest \(x\) and \(y\), as the CQUAD4 element of Nastran:

The element knows neither \(x_1\) nor \(y_1\): its matrices depend on \(l_{ex}\), \(l_{ey}\), \(R\), its stiffnesses and its displacements, not on its position. The mid-surface of the element is curved, \(R\) entering the strains, while its interpolation is written on the developed rectangle.

3.2. Cubic Hermite polynomials#

On \([-1, 1]\), the cubic Hermite polynomials of the end \(\xi_i = \pm 1\) are

(3.2)#\[\phi_i(\xi) = \frac{1}{4} (\xi + \xi_i)^2 (2 - \xi \xi_i), \qquad \psi_i(\xi) = \frac{1}{4} \xi_i (\xi + \xi_i)^2 (\xi \xi_i - 1)\]

\(\phi_i\) is one at its end and zero at the other, with zero slope at both; \(\psi_i\) is zero at both ends, with the slope \(d\psi_i/d\xi\) one at its end and zero at the other. A cubic on \([-1, 1]\) is fully determined by its values and slopes at the two ends, which is the basis of the continuity of the element.

3.3. Interpolation functions#

The two-dimensional functions of node \(i\) are the tensor products of Eq. (3.2), scaled by \(l_{ex}/2\) and \(l_{ey}/2\) such that their nodal derivatives in \(x\) and \(y\), not in \(\xi\) and \(\eta\), are one:

(3.3)#\[H_i = \phi_i(\xi) \phi_i(\eta), \qquad H_{xi} = \frac{l_{ex}}{2} \psi_i(\xi) \phi_i(\eta), \qquad H_{yi} = \frac{l_{ey}}{2} \phi_i(\xi) \psi_i(\eta), \qquad H_{xyi} = \frac{l_{ex} l_{ey}}{4} \psi_i(\xi) \psi_i(\eta)\]

where \(\phi_i(\eta)\) and \(\psi_i(\eta)\) are those of the end \(\eta_i\). These are the functions \(H_i\), \(H_{xi}\), \(H_{yi}\) and \(H_{xyi}\) of the Notes of bfsccylinder.bfsccylinder. At the nodes, \(H_i\) is one at its node and zero at the others, with zero derivatives \(\partial/\partial x\), \(\partial/\partial y\) and \(\partial^2/\partial x \partial y\); the other three functions vanish at all nodes, and so do these derivatives, except \(\partial H_{xi}/\partial x = \partial H_{yi}/\partial y = \partial^2 H_{xyi}/\partial x \partial y = 1\) at their own node. The displacements are

(3.4)#\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} u &= \sum_{i=1}^{4} \left( H_i u_i + H_{xi} {u_{,x}}_i + H_{yi} {u_{,y}}_i \right) \\ v &= \sum_{i=1}^{4} \left( H_i v_i + H_{xi} {v_{,x}}_i + H_{yi} {v_{,y}}_i \right) \\ w &= \sum_{i=1}^{4} \left( H_i w_i + H_{xi} {w_{,x}}_i + H_{yi} {w_{,y}}_i + H_{xyi} {w_{,xy}}_i \right) \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

The 16 functions of \(w\) span the complete bicubic polynomials, \(\xi^a \eta^b\) with \(a, b \leq 3\); the 12 functions of \(u\) and of \(v\) span a subset of them.

The element vector \(\boldsymbol{u}_e\), of size 40, the attribute u, holds the degrees of freedom of the nodes 1, 2, 3 and 4 in sequence, 10 per node in the order of the table. In row form,

(3.5)#\[u = \boldsymbol{S}^u \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad v = \boldsymbol{S}^v \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad w = \boldsymbol{S}^w \boldsymbol{u}_e\]

with the rows of 40 entries evaluated at a point \((\xi, \eta)\) by BFSCCylinder.update_Su(), BFSCCylinder.update_Sv() and BFSCCylinder.update_Sw(), and their derivatives, e.g. \(w_{,x} = \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,x} \boldsymbol{u}_e\), by BFSCCylinder.update_Su_x(), BFSCCylinder.update_Su_y(), BFSCCylinder.update_Sv_x(), BFSCCylinder.update_Sv_y(), BFSCCylinder.update_Sw_x() and BFSCCylinder.update_Sw_y(). The rows of the Sanders-type element, bfsccylinder.bfsccylinder_sanders.BFSCCylinderSanders, are the same. The derivatives as degrees of freedom have a mechanical meaning: \(-w_{,x}\) and \(-w_{,y}\) are the Donnell-type rotations of the normal, \(u_{,x}\) the axial strain and \(u_{,y} + v_{,x}\) the shear strain at the node.

3.5. Continuity between elements#

Along an edge \(x\) = constant, \(\xi = \pm 1\), the functions \(\psi_i(\xi)\) vanish and \(\phi_i(\xi)\) is one for the nodes of the edge and zero for the others, such that \(w\) along the edge is the cubic in \(\eta\) of \(w\) and \(w_{,y}\) of the two nodes of the edge. The normal slope \(w_{,x}\) along the edge has the contributions of \(H_{xi}\) and \(H_{xyi}\) only, since \(d\phi_i/d\xi\) vanishes at \(\xi = \pm 1\): it is the cubic in \(\eta\) of \(w_{,x}\) and \(w_{,xy}\) of the same two nodes. The same holds for the edges \(y\) = constant and for \(u\) and \(v\), whose normal derivatives along an edge depend on \(u_{,x}\), \(v_{,x}\) (or \(u_{,y}\), \(v_{,y}\)) of the nodes of the edge. Two elements that share an edge, and therefore its two nodes, have the same \(u\), \(v\), \(w\) and the same first derivatives along it: the interpolation is \(C^1\) continuous, as required by the second derivatives of \(w\) in the curvatures, and the membrane strains are continuous as well. The continuity requires a structured mesh of rectangles whose sides follow \(x\) and \(y\), with the nodes of the elements that share an edge at the same positions, see Assembling a model.

3.6. Strain operators#

Substituting Eq. (3.5) into the strains of Kinematics of the cylindrical shell gives the operators of the element:

(3.6)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_L = \boldsymbol{B}_m \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad \boldsymbol{B}_m = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{S}^u_{,x} \\ \boldsymbol{S}^v_{,y} + \boldsymbol{S}^w/R \\ \boldsymbol{S}^u_{,y} + \boldsymbol{S}^v_{,x} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]
(3.7)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\kappa} = \boldsymbol{B}_b \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad \boldsymbol{B}_b = \begin{bmatrix} -\boldsymbol{S}^w_{,xx} \\ -\boldsymbol{S}^w_{,yy} + s \, \boldsymbol{S}^v_{,y}/R \\ -2 \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,xy} + s \left( \dfrac{3}{2R} \boldsymbol{S}^v_{,x} - \dfrac{1}{2R} \boldsymbol{S}^u_{,y} \right) \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

with \(s = 0\) for the Donnell-type and \(s = 1\) for the Sanders-type kinematics, evaluated by BFSCCylinder.update_Bm(), BFSCCylinder.update_Bb() and bfsccylinder.bfsccylinder_sanders.BFSCCylinderSanders.update_Bb(). With the rows of the rotations,

(3.8)#\[\begin{split}\begin{Bmatrix} -\beta_x \\ -\beta_y \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{Bmatrix} w_{,x} \\ w_{,y} - k_v v \end{Bmatrix} = \boldsymbol{G} \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad \boldsymbol{G} = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,x} \\ \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,y} - k_v \boldsymbol{S}^v \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

the rows G1 and G2 of the code, the variation of the nonlinear strains of Eq. (1.9) is \(\delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{NL} = \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \delta \boldsymbol{u}_e\), and \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{NL} = \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \boldsymbol{u}_e\), with

(3.9)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{B}_{NL} = \begin{bmatrix} w_{,x} \boldsymbol{G}_1 \\ (w_{,y} - k_v v) \boldsymbol{G}_2 \\ w_{,x} \boldsymbol{G}_2 + (w_{,y} - k_v v) \boldsymbol{G}_1 \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

\(\boldsymbol{G}_1\) and \(\boldsymbol{G}_2\) being the rows of \(\boldsymbol{G}\) and the slopes those of the current displacements; the rows of \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) are BmL1, BmL2 and BmL3 in the code. \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) depends on the displacements, \(\boldsymbol{B}_m\) and \(\boldsymbol{B}_b\) do not.

3.7. Numerical integration#

The integrals over the element are computed with the product Gauss-Legendre rule of bfsccylinder.quadrature.get_points_weights():

(3.10)#\[\int_A f \, dA \approx \frac{l_{ex} l_{ey}}{4} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} w_i w_j f(\xi_i, \eta_j)\]

with \(n\) = nint points \(\xi_i\) in each direction, the roots of the Legendre polynomial of degree \(n\), and weights \(w_i\). The rule is exact for polynomials of degree up to \(2n - 1\) in each of \(\xi\) and \(\eta\). With \(\boldsymbol{A}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}\) and \(\boldsymbol{D}\) constant over the element, the integrands are polynomials, whose degree in each variable gives the number of points of an exact integration:

The tangent stiffness matrix is the exact derivative of the quadrature sums of the internal forces for any nint, see Element matrices and internal forces, such that a reduced integration changes the discrete model but not the consistency of the Newton-Raphson iterations. The same nint is given to the constructor of the element, BFSCCylinder(nint), which sizes the arrays of the stiffnesses, and to bfsccylinder.quadrature.get_points_weights(), whose points and weights are given to the functions.

3.8. Attributes of the element#

Before the functions are called, each element receives:

  • c1, c2, c3, c4: positions of its nodes in the global vectors;

  • lex, ley and R: the lengths of its sides and the radius, R nonzero;

  • A11, …, D66: the stiffnesses at its integration points, see Constitutive relation;

  • h and rho: thickness and density, for the mass matrix only;

  • init_k_KC0, init_k_KCNL, init_k_KG, init_k_M and init_k_KCfollower: positions of its entries in the sparse arrays of each matrix, see Assembling a model.

n1, n2, n3 and n4 are identification numbers for the user, not read by the functions. The methods update_Su, …, update_Bb evaluate the rows and operators at one point, e.g. to recover the strains from a solution; the update_* functions of the modules evaluate the same quantities in local variables at each integration point.

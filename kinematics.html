1. Kinematics of the cylindrical shell#

A thin cylindrical shell can be described by several strain-displacement relations that differ in terms of the order of the thickness over the radius, or of the wavelength of the deformation over the radius. Two of them are implemented, with the same element, nodes, degrees of freedom, interpolation and quadrature:

  • the Donnell-type kinematics [Donnell1933], also called shallow-shell or Donnell-Mushtari-Vlasov kinematics, in which the curvature of the shell enters only the hoop membrane strain;

  • the Sanders-type kinematics, whose linear part is the first-approximation theory of Sanders [Sanders1959] and Koiter [Koiter1960] and whose nonlinear part is that of Sanders [Sanders1963] for small strains and moderate rotations.

This chapter derives both from the same displacement field, compares them and states when each applies. The interpolation is given in The BFSC element on the cylinder and the matrices that follow from the kinematics in Element matrices and internal forces.

1.1. Geometry and coordinates#

The cylinder has the radius \(R\) of its mid-surface and the cylindrical coordinates \((x, \theta, r)\): \(x\) along the axis, \(\theta\) the circumferential angle and \(r\) the distance from the axis. The element uses the arc length along the circumference and the distance from the mid-surface,

(1.1)#\[y = R \theta, \qquad z = r - R\]

with \(z\) positive outwards. The local basis of the mid-surface, \(\boldsymbol{e}_x\), \(\boldsymbol{e}_y\) and the outward normal \(\boldsymbol{e}_z\), is right-handed, \(\boldsymbol{e}_x \times \boldsymbol{e}_y = \boldsymbol{e}_z\), which fixes the sense of \(\theta\), and it turns along \(y\):

(1.2)#\[\boldsymbol{e}_{y,y} = -\frac{1}{R} \boldsymbol{e}_z, \qquad \boldsymbol{e}_{z,y} = \frac{1}{R} \boldsymbol{e}_y\]

The displacements of the mid-surface are \(u\) along \(\boldsymbol{e}_x\) (axial), \(v\) along \(\boldsymbol{e}_y\) (circumferential) and \(w\) along \(\boldsymbol{e}_z\) (normal, positive outwards, away from the axis). The attribute R of the element is \(R\), and its lengths lex and ley are measured along \(x\) and \(y\). Derivatives are written with a comma, \(w_{,x} = \partial w/\partial x\).

1.2. Assumptions common to both kinematics#

  • Thin shell, Kirchhoff-Love hypothesis: the normals to the mid-surface remain straight, normal and unstretched, such that the transverse shear deformation is neglected and the strains vary linearly through the thickness, \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(z) = \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} + z \boldsymbol{\kappa}\).

  • Shallow-shell thickness integration: the factor \(1 + z/R\) of the metric is approximated by one, in the strains and in the volume \(dV = (1 + z/R)\, dz\, dA\), such that the stress resultants are those of a flat laminate, see Constitutive relation.

  • Small strains and moderate rotations: of the nonlinear terms of the Green-Lagrange strains, the membrane strains keep only the squares and products of the rotations of the normal. With the ordering \(u_{,x} \sim \epsilon\) and \(w_{,x} \sim \epsilon^{1/2}\), every term of order \(\epsilon\) is retained.

  • The rotation of the material about the normal does not enter the nonlinear strains.

With the rotations of the normal \(\beta_x\), in the plane \(xz\), and \(\beta_y\), in the plane \(yz\), the displacement field through the thickness is

(1.3)#\[u(x, y, z) = u + z \beta_x, \qquad v(x, y, z) = v + z \beta_y, \qquad w(x, y, z) = w\]

and the two kinematics differ in the rotation \(\beta_y\).

1.3. Rotation of the normal#

A circumferential displacement \(v\) carries a material point along an arc of the circle of radius \(R\), which turns the local frame by \(v/R\), Eq. (1.2). The rotation of the normal about \(x\) is the rotation of the slope of \(w\) plus this rotation of the frame:

(1.4)#\[\begin{split}\beta_x = -w_{,x}, \qquad \beta_y = -w_{,y} + k_v v, \qquad k_v = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{Donnell-type} \\ 1/R & \text{Sanders-type} \end{cases}\end{split}\]

The Donnell-type kinematics neglect the term \(v/R\) and keep the rotations of a flat plate.

1.4. Linear membrane strains#

The linear membrane strains are the same for both kinematics:

(1.5)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_L = \begin{Bmatrix} \varepsilon_{xx} \\ \varepsilon_{yy} \\ \gamma_{xy} \end{Bmatrix}_L = \begin{Bmatrix} u_{,x} \\ v_{,y} + w/R \\ u_{,y} + v_{,x} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

with the engineering shear strain \(\gamma_{xy}\). The curvature of the shell appears in the hoop strain: a uniform \(w\) stretches the circumference by \(w/R\). This term couples the membrane and the bending behaviour of the shell even for a homogeneous wall.

1.5. Curvatures#

The changes of curvature are the derivatives of the rotations. For the Donnell-type kinematics, \(k_v = 0\), they are those of a flat plate:

(1.6)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{D} = \begin{Bmatrix} \kappa_{xx} \\ \kappa_{yy} \\ \kappa_{xy} \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{Bmatrix} -w_{,xx} \\ -w_{,yy} \\ -2 w_{,xy} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

For the Sanders-type kinematics, the term \(v/R\) of \(\beta_y\) adds \(v_{,y}/R\) to \(\kappa_{yy}\) and \(v_{,x}/R\) to the twist. The rotation of the material about the normal,

(1.7)#\[\omega = \frac{1}{2} \left( v_{,x} - u_{,y} \right)\]

is not a rotation of the normal and cannot be produced by a change of \(\beta_y\); Sanders’ twist [Sanders1959] adds it, divided by \(R\), such that the twist vanishes for every small rigid-body motion. The result is

(1.8)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{S} = \begin{Bmatrix} -w_{,xx} \\ -w_{,yy} + \dfrac{1}{R} v_{,y} \\ -2 w_{,xy} + \dfrac{3}{2R} v_{,x} - \dfrac{1}{2R} u_{,y} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

where \(3 v_{,x}/(2R) = v_{,x}/R + v_{,x}/(2R)\) collects the term of \(\beta_y\) and the term of \(\omega/R\). The curvatures of both kinematics are linear in the displacements.

1.6. Nonlinear membrane strains#

For moderate rotations, the nonlinear membrane strains are the products of the rotations of the normal, Eq. (1.4):

(1.9)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{NL} = \begin{Bmatrix} \beta_x^2/2 \\ \beta_y^2/2 \\ \beta_x \beta_y \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{Bmatrix} w_{,x}^2/2 \\ (w_{,y} - k_v v)^2/2 \\ w_{,x} (w_{,y} - k_v v) \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

For the Donnell-type kinematics these are the von Kármán terms of a flat plate. For the Sanders-type kinematics [Sanders1963], \(\beta_y^2/2 = w_{,y}^2/2 + v^2/(2R^2) - v w_{,y}/R\), and the terms in \(v\) are those of the Green-Lagrange strains in curvilinear coordinates that the Donnell-type kinematics neglect. The term \(\omega^2/2\) of the rotation about the normal, Eq. (1.7), is neglected in both. The membrane strains are

(1.10)#\[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_L + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{NL}\]

and the curvatures remain linear. The rotations of Eq. (1.4) enter three other places, which follow the kinematics chosen: the geometric stiffness matrix, Eq. (4.8), the rotary inertia of the mass matrix, Eq. (4.10), and the area vector of a follower pressure, Eq. (5.2).

1.7. The rigid-body criterion#

Strain measures must vanish for a rigid-body motion. A small translation of the cross-section by \(c\), perpendicular to the axis, is

(1.11)#\[u = 0, \qquad v = -c \sin(y/R), \qquad w = c \cos(y/R)\]

for which \(\varepsilon_{yy} = v_{,y} + w/R = 0\) for both kinematics, but

\[\kappa_{yy}^{D} = -w_{,yy} = \frac{w}{R^2} \neq 0, \qquad \kappa_{yy}^{S} = -w_{,yy} + \frac{v_{,y}}{R} = 0\]

and likewise the Donnell-type rotation \(\beta_y = -w_{,y} = c \sin(y/R)/R\) does not vanish, while the Sanders-type rotation \(-w_{,y} + v/R\) does. The Donnell-type kinematics predict a bending strain of order \(c/R^2\) in a shell that has not deformed; the Sanders-type strains and curvatures vanish for every small rigid-body motion, the defining property of the Sanders-Koiter theory [Sanders1959].

1.8. Range of validity#

Both kinematics share the limits of the assumptions above: thin walls, \(h/R \ll 1\), without transverse shear deformation; small strains and moderate rotations; a linear elastic material, see Constitutive relation. Within these limits, the Donnell-type kinematics add the approximation that the deformation varies over lengths small compared with \(R\). For an inextensional mode with \(n\) circumferential waves, \(v_{,y} = -w/R\), the neglected term of \(\kappa_{yy}\) is \(|v_{,y}/R| / |w_{,yy}| = 1/n^2\) of the retained one, and the term \(v/R\) of \(\beta_y\) is \(1/n^2\) of \(w_{,y}\). The Donnell-type kinematics are therefore accurate when \(1/n^2 \ll 1\), in practice for \(n \geq 4\) or \(5\) circumferential waves [Amabili2008], and lose accuracy for few waves [Leissa1973]. For laminated panels under axial compression, the error of the Donnell-type kinematics also depends on the anisotropy of the laminate and on the ratios \(R/h\) and \(L/R\) [JaunkyKnight1999].

Two results show the size of the difference. For a ring under a uniform follower pressure buckling with \(n = 2\) waves, the inextensional buckling pressure is \(p R^3/D = -3\) with the Sanders-type kinematics and \(-16/5\) with the Donnell-type kinematics, \(D\) being the bending stiffness of the wall, see Follower pressure. Under axial compression with a membrane prebuckling state, \(N_{yy} = N_{xy} = 0\), the geometric stiffness matrices of the two kinematics coincide, Eq. (4.8), and the difference comes only from the curvatures in \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\): for a laminated cylinder of ten plies with \(R/h = 200\) and \(L/R \approx 2\), clamped at both ends and under a constant axial membrane force, the critical loads of the two elements differ by about 0.6 %.

1.9. How to choose#

  • The Sanders-type kinematics are the general choice: they are correct for rigid-body motions and apply to modes with few circumferential waves, e.g. rings, long cylinders under external pressure or torsion, the lowest natural frequencies. They cost only more nonzero entries in \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\), \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\), \(\boldsymbol{M}\) and the load stiffness matrix of the follower pressure, see Element matrices and internal forces.

  • The Donnell-type kinematics are adequate for short-wavelength, membrane-dominated behaviour, e.g. the buckling of a cylinder under axial compression, whose critical modes have many waves, and when a comparison with the classical shallow-shell results is wanted.

  • The same kinematics must be used for every matrix of an analysis: the stiffness and the geometric stiffness matrices of a buckling problem, or the internal forces and the tangent stiffness matrix of a nonlinear analysis, belong to one strain measure. Taking every function from one namespace guarantees it.

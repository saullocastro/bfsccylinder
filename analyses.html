7. Analyses#

The element gives the matrices and vectors of Element matrices and internal forces; the global systems are assembled as in Assembling a model and solved with SciPy or with structsolve. This chapter derives the systems of the four analyses from the equilibrium of the shell, for either kinematics, and states what each assumes.

7.1. Equilibrium#

With the global displacement vector \(\boldsymbol{u}\), the internal forces \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u})\) of update_fint(), the gradient of the strain energy, and the external forces \(\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) of a reference load scaled by the load multiplier \(\lambda\), the principle of virtual work gives the residual

(7.1)#\[\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{u}, \lambda) = \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u}) - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

over the unknown degrees of freedom, with the Jacobian \(\boldsymbol{K}_T(\boldsymbol{u})\) of Eq. (4.4). For a follower pressure, \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) depends on \(\boldsymbol{u}\) and the Jacobian has the load stiffness matrix as well, Eq. (5.7). The analyses below are this equation, its linearisation, or its stability.

7.2. Linear static analysis#

For small displacements, \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int} = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u}\) and

(7.2)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\]

with \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) of update_KC0(), symmetric and positive definite once the rigid-body motions are constrained. The degrees of freedom are partitioned into the unknown ones, \(u\), and the constrained ones, \(k\), with the known values \(\bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\), zero for a support and nonzero for a prescribed displacement:

(7.3)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{uu} \boldsymbol{u}_u = \boldsymbol{F}_u - \boldsymbol{K}_{uk} \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_k = \boldsymbol{K}_{uk}^\top \boldsymbol{u}_u + \boldsymbol{K}_{kk} \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\]

with \(\boldsymbol{F}_k\) the reaction forces. A prescribed displacement, e.g. the axial shortening of one end, is the load of the analysis, and the sum of its reactions is the corresponding force. A pressure enters through the force vector of update_fext_follower() at \(\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\), the consistent load vector of a dead pressure.

7.3. Linear buckling analysis#

A load \(\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) produces, in the linear range, the prebuckling state \(\lambda \boldsymbol{u}_0\), with \(\boldsymbol{u}_0\) the linear static solution of the reference load. The equilibrium becomes neutral, and the shell buckles, when the tangent stiffness matrix of this state is singular. Since \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) is homogeneous of degree one in the displacements, \(\boldsymbol{K}_G(\lambda \boldsymbol{u}_0) = \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u}_0)\), and neglecting the terms of the prebuckling rotations in \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\), which vanish for a membrane prebuckling state, the tangent stiffness matrix is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u}_0)\) and

(7.4)#\[\left( \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G \right) \boldsymbol{\phi} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

The eigenvalues \(\lambda\) are the load multipliers of the reference load, the critical load being the one of smallest magnitude, and the eigenvectors \(\boldsymbol{\phi}\) the buckling modes. \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) is computed in one of two ways:

  • update_KG() from \(\boldsymbol{u}_0\), with the resultants of the linear strains at each integration point, Eq. (4.8). They are the resultants of the shell with its boundary conditions, including the bending boundary layers near the supports, the hoop resultants that a restrained Poisson expansion creates, and the effect of an unbalanced laminate. For a prescribed displacement, \(\lambda\) multiplies the sum of its reactions;

  • update_KG_constant_stress() from given membrane resultants \(N_{xx}\), \(N_{yy}\) and \(N_{xy}\), without a static analysis. \(\lambda\) multiplies these resultants, e.g. \(N_{xx} = -1\) for an axial compression of total force \(-2 \pi R\) along the circumference. The prebuckling state is then assumed to be a membrane state, and the boundary conditions enter the problem only through \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\).

\(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) and \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) are both symmetric, and the problem is a generalised symmetric eigenvalue problem, e.g. solved for the lowest load multipliers as \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{\phi} = \lambda (-\boldsymbol{K}_G) \boldsymbol{\phi}\), or with structsolve.lb, which solves Eq. (7.4). Under a follower pressure the load stiffness matrix of the reference pressure is added to \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\), Eq. (5.9), and the problem is unsymmetric in general. The two matrices must come from the same kinematics, see Kinematics of the cylindrical shell. Linear buckling neglects the nonlinearity of the prebuckling path, which the nonlinear static analysis below takes into account, and the imperfections of the shell; the element describes a perfect cylinder.

7.4. Natural frequencies#

The kinetic energy of Eq. (4.9) and the strain energy linearised about the undeformed state give the equations of free vibration, \(\boldsymbol{M} \ddot{\boldsymbol{u}} + \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\), whose harmonic solutions \(\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{\phi} \sin(\omega_n t)\) solve

(7.5)#\[\left( \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} - \omega_n^2 \boldsymbol{M} \right) \boldsymbol{\phi} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

with the consistent mass matrix \(\boldsymbol{M}\) of update_M(), the natural angular frequencies \(\omega_n\), in radians per unit time, and the modes \(\boldsymbol{\phi}\). Both matrices are symmetric, \(\boldsymbol{M}\) positive definite. The lowest frequencies of a cylinder are clustered, close to each other, and those of a closed cylinder come in pairs of equal frequencies, the modes \(\cos(n\theta)\) and \(\sin(n\theta)\) of the same number \(n\) of circumferential waves. A solver of the lowest eigenpairs must therefore be verified for missing eigenvalues, e.g. with a shift-invert iteration about zero and the check of the residual of each eigenpair. Since the lowest modes may have few waves, the Sanders-type kinematics are the safer choice for them, see Kinematics of the cylindrical shell.

7.5. Nonlinear static analysis#

For large displacements, Eq. (7.1) is solved for \(\boldsymbol{u}\) with the nonlinear strains of the kinematics, e.g. with the Newton-Raphson method under load control:

(7.6)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_T(\boldsymbol{u}^{(i)}) \Delta \boldsymbol{u} = -\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{u}^{(i)}, \lambda), \qquad \boldsymbol{u}^{(i+1)} = \boldsymbol{u}^{(i)} + \Delta \boldsymbol{u}\]

with the load multiplier \(\lambda\) increased in steps, \(\lambda = 1\) being the full load, and

(7.7)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_T = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} (\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u})\]

of update_KC0(), update_KCNL() and update_KG(), all computed with the current displacements and with the same integration points as \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\). \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) is the exact Jacobian of \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\), such that the iterations converge quadratically near the solution. A follower pressure adds its force vector to the external forces and its load stiffness matrix to \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\), Eq. (5.7). Past a limit point of the load, load control fails, and an arc-length method, in which \(\lambda\) is an unknown constrained together with \(\boldsymbol{u}\) [Crisfield1981], follows the equilibrium path.

The degrees of freedom mix displacements, their first derivatives and \(w_{,xy}\), of different dimensions. A convergence criterion on the Euclidean norm of \(\boldsymbol{R}\) depends on the units. Scaling each component of the residual and of the reference forces with the diagonal entry of a stiffness matrix, as in the convergence test of Crisfield [Crisfield1981], removes this dependence; over the free degrees of freedom it also leaves out the reactions at the constrained ones.

7.6. Limit points, bifurcations and imperfections#

In the Riks method [Riks1979] the load multiplier is an unknown, and the increment of \((\boldsymbol{u}, \lambda)\) of each step is constrained to a prescribed arc length, such that the path is followed through the limit points of the load, where \(\lambda\) reaches a maximum or a minimum and load control fails. At a critical point of the path, a limit point or a bifurcation, \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) is singular: one of its eigenvalues crosses zero. The number of negative eigenvalues of \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) at the converged states therefore locates the critical points crossed: it is zero on a stable branch, increases by one at each limit point beyond which the branch is unstable and at each bifurcation crossed without branching, and the sign of the determinant of \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) changes each time an odd number of eigenvalues crosses zero. For a symmetric \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) the count is the number of negative pivots of a symmetric factorization; for the unsymmetric \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) of a nonconservative follower pressure it is the number of negative real eigenvalues among those of smallest magnitude. A path that keeps a symmetry of the perfect shell, e.g. the axisymmetric path of an axially compressed cylinder, crosses bifurcations whose modes break that symmetry; the bifurcated branch is reached by switching to it along the critical eigenvector, after the change of the count of negative eigenvalues [WagnerWriggers1988].

The element describes a perfect cylinder. An initial imperfection \(w_0\) of the normal displacement, with the degrees of freedom \(\boldsymbol{u}_0\), can be added exactly when the non-linear membrane strains are quadratic forms of the rotations, as with the Donnell-type kinematics: the imperfect shell has the membrane strains \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u} + \boldsymbol{u}_0) - \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_0)\) and the curvatures \(\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{u})\), which gives

(7.8)#\[\boldsymbol{F}_{int,imp}(\boldsymbol{u}) = \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u} + \boldsymbol{u}_0) - \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u}_0) - \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{N}_0) \boldsymbol{u}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_{T,imp}(\boldsymbol{u}) = \boldsymbol{K}_T(\boldsymbol{u} + \boldsymbol{u}_0) - \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{N}_0)\]

with \(\boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{N}_0)\) the geometric stiffness matrix of the membrane resultants \(\boldsymbol{N}_0\) of the whole strains of \(\boldsymbol{u}_0\), linear and non-linear. update_KG() integrates the resultants of the linear strains only, such that the part of the non-linear strains of \(\boldsymbol{u}_0\) must be added to it, with the same integration points. The imperfect shell is stress free at \(\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\), and an imperfection in the shape of the lowest buckling mode turns the bifurcation of the perfect shell into a limit point.

7.7. Solving with structsolve#

The solvers of structsolve receive the matrices and vectors of the model, assembled by the user; it is not a requirement of bfsccylinder, see Installing bfsccylinder. The linear buckling problem is solved by structsolve.lb, with symmetric=False when it contains the load stiffness matrix of a follower pressure.

The nonlinear static analysis is solved by structsolve.Analysis, with four callables that loop over the elements, call the functions of either kinematics and assemble:

  • calc_fext(inc=1., silent=False), the external forces \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) times the load factor inc;

  • calc_fint(c, silent=False), the internal forces of update_fint() at the displacements c;

  • calc_kC(c=None, NLgeom=False, silent=False), \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) with NLgeom=False, and \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} (\boldsymbol{c}) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{c})\) with NLgeom=True;

  • calc_kG(c=None, NLgeom=False, silent=False), \(\boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{c})\) of update_KG();

such that calc_kC + calc_kG is \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) of Eq. (7.7) and the residual of structsolve is Eq. (7.1). The value arrays of the sparse matrices are zeroed in each call, since the functions add to them. Analysis.static increases the load factor from 0 to 1, the first increment of size Analysis.initialInc and the next ones growing up to Analysis.maxInc, and returns the load factors and the solutions of the converged increments. Analysis.NL_method selects the Newton-Raphson method, 'NR', or an arc-length method, and the convergence criterion is Crisfield’s by default, with the tolerance Analysis.relTOL = 1e-9; Analysis.convergence = 'relative' selects the Euclidean norm instead.

structsolve imposes no boundary condition itself. The constrained degrees of freedom are removed either by the partition of Eq. (7.3), the callables receiving and returning the vectors and matrices of the unknown degrees of freedom, or with null rows and columns: the callables return the vectors and matrices of the whole model with the rows and columns of the constrained degrees of freedom zeroed, which the solvers remove.

A follower pressure \(\lambda p_0\) enters through the keyword argument inc, the load factor, which structsolve passes to the callables that declare it: the external forces are the dead part \(\boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{0})\) of \(p_0\), calc_fint subtracts the part that depends on the displacements, \(\lambda (\boldsymbol{F}_p (\boldsymbol{u}) - \boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{0}))\) of update_fext_follower() with reference=0, and calc_kC adds the load stiffness matrix of \(\lambda p_0\). The tangent stiffness matrix is then unsymmetric in general, which the solvers support with LU factorisations. For the arc-length methods, calc_fext also declares the configuration c and returns the follower force vector \(\boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{c})\) of \(p_0\), minus the derivative of the residual with respect to the load factor.

Torsional buckling of ring-stiffened cylinders: Moore and Wescoat (1944), Table I#

Moore and Wescoat [1] tested in torsion 24S-T aluminium-alloy cylinders of mean diameter \(D = 30.08\) in, overall length 36 in and sheet thickness \(t = 0.020\) in, without stiffeners (specimen 16) and with \(1/2 \times 1/2\) in solid ring stiffeners inside the skin (specimens 17, 18, 13 and 13A), and gave in Table I (p. 25) the average observed shear-buckling stress and the theoretical one, from Donnell’s formula for hinged edges [2]. This notebook computes the first buckling stress of the perfect cylinders with the BFSC cylinder element of Sanders-type kinematics (bfsccylinder.sanders.BFSCCylinderSanders) and compares it with both columns of Table I. The stiffened specimens 19, 14, 20, 15 and 21 of the same report (Table II) are the subject of the notebook moore1944_stiffened_cylinders.ipynb.

Pages are the printed ones of [1] (pdf page = printed page + 2).

References

  1. Moore, R. L., and Wescoat, C., Torsion tests of stiffened circular cylinders, NACA ARR 4E31 (Wartime Report W-89), 1944.

  2. Donnell, L. H., Stability of thin-walled tubes under torsion, NACA Report 479, 1933 (reference 6 of [1]; formula quoted in the appendix of [1], p. 23).

Membrane state and Donnell’s formula#

A torque \(T\) on a perfect circular cylinder gives a uniform membrane shear force in the skin, the same on every section \(x\) = constant by equilibrium,

\[N_{xy} = \frac{T}{2 \pi R^2}, \qquad \tau = \frac{N_{xy}}{t} = \frac{T}{2 \pi R^2 t} = \frac{24\, T_{\mathrm{ft\,lb}}}{\pi D^2 t}\]

the last form being that of [1] (Fig. 5, note 6: \(\tau = 0.422\, T\) for \(t = 0.020\) in). The rings rotate rigidly with their section and carry no membrane force. The constant \(N_{xy}\) is therefore the exact pre-buckling membrane state, and the geometric stiffness matrix is that of a constant \(N_{xy}\) (update_KG_constant_stress); this is checked below against the geometric stiffness matrix from a linear static analysis with a prescribed end rotation.

The theoretical column of Table I is Donnell’s formula for hinged ends [2], as written in the appendix of [1], p. 23, valid for \(L^2 t/D^3 < 5.2\):

\[\tau_0 = \frac{E t^2}{(1 - \nu^2) L^2} \left[2.8 + \sqrt{2.6 + 1.4 \left(\sqrt{1 - \nu^2}\, \frac{L^2}{t D}\right)^{3/2}}\right]\]

with \(L\) the length of the buckled section, centre to centre of the rings (Table I). Reproduced:

Model#

moore1944_utils.MooreSector builds a periodic sector of the cylinder on the sector model of channel_cylinder_sector.py (skin of BFSCCylinderSanders elements, 10 degrees of freedom per node, cyclic symmetry by a constraint basis \(\boldsymbol{Z}_j\)).

Cyclic symmetry. A ring-only cylinder is axisymmetric: its buckling modes are \(e^{i n \theta}\) around the circumference, one problem per wave number \(n\). The model is a thin slice of angle \(\phi = 2\pi/n_s\) (\(n_s\) slices, two elements across) whose edge \(\theta = \phi\) is tied to the edge \(\theta = 0\) by \(\boldsymbol{u}(\phi) = e^{i n \phi} \boldsymbol{u}(0)\) (Bloch index \(j = n\)). The reduced matrices \(\boldsymbol{Z}_n^H \boldsymbol{K} \boldsymbol{Z}_n\) are Hermitian; \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is positive definite after the boundary conditions and the lowest positive multipliers \(\lambda\) of \((\boldsymbol{K} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G) \boldsymbol{x} = 0\) are the largest eigenvalues \(1/\lambda\) of \(\boldsymbol{K}^{-1}(-\boldsymbol{K}_G)\). With \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) of \(N_{xy} = 1\) lb/in, \(\lambda\) is the critical \(N_{xy}\) and \(\tau_{cr} = \lambda/t\). Under torsion the multipliers of an isotropic shell come in pairs \(\pm\lambda\) (a helical mode and its mirror image, here in the same \(n\)): the sign of the torque does not matter.

Rings. The finite element library has no beam element. A ring is a band of skin elements of axial width \(b_r = 1/2\) in centred at the ring station (two elements across), whose laminate is the skin plus a layer of thickness \(d_r = 1/2\) in on the inner face, \(-t/2 - d_r \le z \le -t/2\), with \(\boldsymbol{A}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}\), \(\boldsymbol{D}\) referred to the middle surface of the skin (the base layer of the channel stringer of channel_cylinder_sector.TwoLayerSkin, on the other face). With the Poisson’s ratio of the ring layer set to zero, \(Q_{11} = Q_{22} = E\), the band has the axial stiffness \(E b_r d_r\) of the bar, its bending stiffness about the skin middle surface, \(E (I_r + b_r d_r e^2)\), \(e = (t + d_r)/2\), i.e. the bending of the bar normal to the skin including its eccentricity, and, through the membrane stiffness of the band and the cubic in-plane interpolation across it, the bending of the bar in the plane of the ring section (about the radial axis). The continuous bond is the perfect bond of the Kirchhoff kinematics across the 0.5 in layer: the normal of the skin stays normal in the ring, an approximation for a layer 25 times thicker than the skin; the torsional stiffness of the band, \(G b_r d_r^3/3\), is 2.4 times the St Venant value of the square bar, \(0.141\, G d_r^4\) (sensitivity below). The 3/16 in pins of [1] are replaced by the continuous bond.

Boundary conditions. The ends are attached to rigid bulkheads; [1] does not say whether they act as hinged or clamped edges (its theory assumes hinged edges, and p. 9 estimates clamped edges to raise it by 10 to 12 %). The baseline is hinged: \(u = v = w = 0\) and their derivatives along the edge at both ends, \(w_{,x}\) free; the sensitivity “clamped” adds \(w_{,x} = w_{,xy} = 0\). The sensitivity “rigid rings” adds \(v = w = 0\) (and \(v_{,y} = w_{,y} = 0\)) at the line of the ring centres.

Mesh convergence#

dx is the largest element length along \(x\) between the ring bands, nslices the number of slices around the circumference (element arc \(\pi R/n_s\)) and nring the number of elements across a ring band. The wave numbers \(n = 4\) to 40 are scanned and the lowest \(\tau_{cr}\) is kept.

[4]:

specs = ['16', '17', '18', '13', '13A']
NS = range(4, 41)


def lowest(spec, mesh, ends='hinged', rigid_rings=False, **kw):
    dx, nslices, nring = mesh
    m = mu.MooreSector(spec, dx, nslices=nslices, nring=nring, **kw)
    res = m.scan(NS, ends=ends, rigid_rings=rigid_rings)
    n = min(res, key=lambda j: res[j][0])
    return dict(model=m, n=n, tau=res[n][0]/m.t, mode=res[n][1],
                taus={j: res[j][0]/m.t for j in res})

Cross-check: torque from a prescribed end rotation#

For specimen 18 the end \(x = L\) is rotated about the axis by a small angle \(\varphi\) (\(v = R\varphi\), \(v_{,y} = 0\) at its nodes, the other conditions hinged), the linear static problem is solved for the axisymmetric index \(n = 0\), the torque \(T\) is \(R\) times the sum of the reactions on the prescribed \(v\) times \(n_s\), and the geometric stiffness matrix is computed from the displacements (update_KG, membrane forces of every integration point from the laminate stiffnesses and the strains). The buckling torque of the lowest wave number is compared with that of the constant-\(N_{xy}\) matrix.

Sensitivities#

On the converged mesh: clamped ends; hinged ends with the axial displacement \(u\) free (only \(v = w = 0\) and their derivatives along the edge); rigid ring supports (hinged ends); torsional stiffness of the ring band reduced to the St Venant value of the square bar (shear modulus of the ring layer times \(0.141 \cdot 3 = 0.42\)); Poisson’s ratio of the ring layer \(1/3\) instead of 0; Donnell-type kinematics (bfsccylinder.BFSCCylinder) instead of Sanders-type.

Discussion#

  • Specimen 16 (no rings) checks the model against Donnell’s formula: with \(u = v = w = 0\) at the ends the converged \(\tau_{cr}\) is 5.9 % above Moore’s theoretical value; with the axial displacement free at the ends (“hinged, u free”) it is 1.7 % below it. The restraint of \(u\) is the main difference between the hinged ends of the model and those of Donnell’s solution; the bulkheads of the tests, screwed to the shell, restrain it.

  • Ends. Clamping the ends (\(w_{,x} = 0\)) changes \(\tau_{cr}\) by 0.3 % at most: the 10 to 12 % that [1] (p. 9) expects from clamped edges is not found once \(u\) and \(v\) are restrained at the ends (a sensitivity on \(u\) is in the table above).

  • Rings. The ring bands behave as nearly rigid supports: rigid ring supports change \(\tau_{cr}\) by 0.1 % at most, and the torsional stiffness and the Poisson’s ratio of the ring layer by 0.2 % at most. The buckling stress follows the bay length, as Moore’s use of Donnell’s formula with the length “center to center of ring stiffeners” assumes, and the finite element values of specimens 17, 18, 13 and 13A are within \(-2.1\) to \(+2.1\) % of Moore’s theoretical values. The modes (figure and printed extent, where \(|w|\) exceeds half its largest value) occupy the 27 in bay of specimen 17, the two 13.5 in bays of specimen 18, the two middle 9 in bays of specimen 13A and all four bays of specimen 13, with the largest \(|w|\) in an end bay; [1] reports the end sections of specimen 13 buckled.

  • Tests. The observed stresses are 10 to 32 % below the finite element values of the perfect shell, as they are 7 to 23 % below Moore’s theory (Table I, ratios 0.77 to 0.93): initial imperfections of the thin sheet and the onset of buckling read from the tests lower the measured values.

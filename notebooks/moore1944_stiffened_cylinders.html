Torsional buckling of ring- and stringer-stiffened cylinders: Moore and Wescoat (1944), Table II#

Moore and Wescoat [1] tested in torsion 24S-T cylinders of mean diameter \(D = 30.08\) in with \(1/2 \times 1/2\) in solid rings inside the skin and 8 or 16 channel stringers outside (specimens 19, 14, 20, 15 and 21), and gave in Table II (p. 26) the mean observed shear stress at which the panels between rings and stringers buckled, panel by panel, with the values computed from four theories: ANC-5 [2], Kromm [3], Ebner [4] and Donnell [5] (the last for an unstiffened cylinder of the length of the bay). This notebook computes the first buckling stress of the perfect stiffened cylinders with the BFSC cylinder element of Sanders-type kinematics for the skin and BFSC plate elements for the stringers, and compares it with the lowest panel value of Table II and with the four theories. The ring-only specimens of the same report (Table I) are the subject of the notebook moore1944_ring_cylinders.ipynb, whose model of the rings, boundary conditions and pre-buckling state are used here.

Pages are the printed ones of [1] (pdf page = printed page + 2).

References

  1. Moore, R. L., and Wescoat, C., Torsion tests of stiffened circular cylinders, NACA ARR 4E31 (Wartime Report W-89), 1944.

  2. Strength of Aircraft Elements, Army-Navy-Civil Committee on Aircraft Design Criteria (ANC-5), 1942 (reference 1 of [1]).

  3. Kromm, A., The limit of stability of a curved plate strip under shear and axial stresses, NACA TM 898, 1939 (reference 3 of [1]).

  4. Ebner, H., The strength of shell bodies - theory and practice, NACA TM 838, 1937 (reference 4 of [1]).

  5. Donnell, L. H., Stability of thin-walled tubes under torsion, NACA Report 479, 1933 (reference 6 of [1]).

The stringer section#

The model of the channel (channel_cylinder_sector.ChannelCylinderSector) has a base on the skin, two webs normal to the skin and a flat lip at the free edge of each web, bent at 90 degrees away from the channel, all of thickness \(t_c = 0.032\) in:

  • the base is the outer layer of a two-layer laminate skin + base between the webs, \(b = 0.5 - 0.032 = 0.468\) in between the middle surfaces of the webs (outer width 1/2 in, Fig. 1);

  • the webs go from the middle surface of the skin to the middle surface of the lips, \(H_w = t/2 + 0.64 - 0.032/2\) in (overall height 0.64 in from the outer surface of the skin);

  • the curled lips of Fig. 30 are replaced by flat lips. Their width \(b_l\), from the middle surface of the web to the free edge, is chosen so that the area of the section (middle lines, square corners) is the \(A = 0.072\) in\(^2\) of Fig. 30: \(b_l = (A/t_c - b - 2(0.64 - t_c))/2 = 0.283\) in. The overall width is then \(b + 2 b_l + t_c = 1.07\) in against 1.12 in.

Model#

moore1944_utils.MooreSector with the channel: a sector of one stringer pitch, \(\phi = 2\pi/n_s\), the stringer at its middle, the edge \(\theta = \phi\) tied to \(\theta = 0\) by \(\boldsymbol{u}(\phi) = e^{i j \phi} \boldsymbol{u}(0)\), one Hermitian problem per Bloch index \(j = 0, 1, \ldots, n_s/2\) (indices \(j\) and \(n_s - j\) have the same multipliers). The index \(j\) contains the circumferential harmonics \(n = |j + k n_s|\) coupled by the stringers. The torque gives the constant membrane shear \(N_{xy} = T/(2\pi R^2)\) in the skin, the stringers and the rings unloaded (the exact membrane state, checked with a prescribed end rotation in the ring-only notebook); the geometric stiffness matrix is that of the skin elements with a constant \(N_{xy}\), so the plates of the stringers have none. The lowest positive multiplier over all indices is the first buckling stress \(\tau_{cr} = N_{xy}/t\).

  • Rings as in the ring-only notebook: a band of skin elements of axial width 1/2 in whose laminate is the skin, the ring layer of 1/2 in on the inner face (\(\nu = 0\)) and, under the stringer, the base on the outer face.

  • Stringers: continuous bond of the base to the skin (the two-layer laminate) and rigid 90-degree corners web-base and web-lip; the single row of rivets is not modelled.

  • Ends: hinged (baseline), \(u = v = w = 0\) and their derivatives along the edge for the skin, and the displacements \(U = V = W = 0\) and their derivatives along the edge for the webs and lips (the stringers attached to the bulkheads); clamped adds \(w_{,x} = w_{,xy} = 0\) (\(W_{,X} = W_{,XY} = 0\) of the plates). Rigid rings: \(v = w = 0\) at the line of the ring centres.

Mesh convergence#

A mesh is (dx, da, nweb, nlip): largest element length along \(x\) between the ring bands, largest element arc of the skin between the stringers (nbay elements from the sector edge to a web), elements along a web and across a lip; two elements across the base and across a ring band. All Bloch indices of each specimen are computed, in parallel.

specs = ['19', '14', '20', '15', '21']
NPROC = 8


def nbay(spec, da):
    ns = mu.SPECIMENS[spec]['ns']
    half = np.pi*mu.R/ns - (mu.CH_BASE - mu.CH_T)/2
    return int(np.ceil(half/da))


def tasks(spec, mesh, **kw):
    dx, da, nweb, nlip = mesh
    ns = mu.SPECIMENS[spec]['ns']
    out = []
    for j in range(ns//2 + 1):
        d = dict(spec=spec, dx=dx, nbay=nbay(spec, da), js=[j])
        if kw.get('model') != 'band':
            d.update(nweb=nweb, nlip=nlip)
        d.update(kw)
        out.append(d)
    return out


def run(groups):
    # groups: dict key -> list of task dictionaries; returns key -> result
    flat = [(key, t) for key, ts in groups.items() for t in ts]
    with Pool(NPROC) as pool:
        res = pool.map(mu.lowest, [t for _, t in flat])
    out = {}
    for (key, _), (kw, r) in zip(flat, res):
        o = out.setdefault(key, dict(tau={}, time=0., ndof=0))
        j = list(r['tau'])[0]
        o['tau'][j] = r['tau'][j]
        o['time'] += r['time']
        o['ndof'] = max(o['ndof'], r['ndof'])
        if 'tau_min' not in o or r['tau_min'] < o['tau_min']:
            o.update(tau_min=r['tau_min'], j=j, x=r['x'], theta=r['theta'], w=r['w'])
    return out

Discussion#

  • Convergence. The last refinement changes \(\tau_{cr}\) by 0.26 to 0.31 %; the sensitivities, on the second mesh, are changes of less than 0.1 % (ends, rigid rings, lip width), far below the mesh error.

  • Stringers. Without stringers the same skin and rings buckle 10 to 14 % lower with 8 stringers and 33 to 43 % lower with 16. The multipliers of the different Bloch indices are within 0.4 % (8 stringers) and 4 % (16 stringers) of each other: the stringers act nearly as nodal lines of panel modes, and the lowest mode (figure) is a buckling of the skin panels between rings and stringers. The skin-band representation of the stringers (axial and bending stiffness normal to the skin only, no web or lip plates) is 0.5 to 0.9 % below the discrete channel with 8 stringers and 3 to 5 % below with 16; the band has neither the lateral bending nor the torsional stiffness of the channel.

  • Ends and rings. Clamped ends and rigid ring supports change \(\tau_{cr}\) by 0.1 % at most: the rings bound the panels, as in the ring-only specimens.

  • Lips. Flat lips of the area of Fig. 30 (0.283 in) or of its overall width (0.31 in) give the same \(\tau_{cr}\) to 0.1 %: the section idealisation of the curls does not matter for the first buckling.

  • Tests. The observed stresses of the lowest panel are 6 to 38 % below the finite element values of the perfect shell (14 and 21: 6 and 7 %; 19, 15 and 20: 24, 22 and 38 %). The tests include initial imperfections, the riveted attachment of the stringers through a single row of rivets (continuous bond here) and the reading of the onset of buckling.

  • Moore’s theories. For the 8-stringer specimens Ebner’s values are the closest to the finite element values (finite element 13 and 6 % above them for 19 and 14), followed by Donnell’s unstiffened cylinder of the bay length (19 and 13 % above). For the 16-stringer specimens all the theories of Table II are 34 to 49 % below the finite element values; in the model the base of the channel, bonded over 1/2 in, narrows the panel and restrains its edges, and Donnell’s value ignores the stringers (Table II, footnote).

2. Constitutive relation#

The wall of the shell is a laminate of linear elastic orthotropic plies, described by the classical laminated plate theory [Reddy2003]. Both kinematics of Kinematics of the cylindrical shell use the same constitutive relation; only the strains and curvatures that enter it differ.

2.1. Stress resultants#

With the Kirchhoff-Love strains \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(z) = \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} + z \boldsymbol{\kappa}\) and the plane-stress stiffness \(\bar{\boldsymbol{Q}}(z)\) of the ply at \(z\), in the axes \(x\), \(y\) of the shell, the membrane stress resultants \(\boldsymbol{N} = \{N_{xx}, N_{yy}, N_{xy}\}^\top\) (force per unit length) and the moment resultants \(\boldsymbol{M} = \{M_{xx}, M_{yy}, M_{xy}\}^\top\) (force times length per unit length) are

(2.1)#\[\begin{split}\begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{N} \\ \boldsymbol{M} \end{Bmatrix} = \int_{-h/2}^{h/2} \begin{Bmatrix} 1 \\ z \end{Bmatrix} \bar{\boldsymbol{Q}}(z) \left( \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} + z \boldsymbol{\kappa} \right) dz = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{A} & \boldsymbol{B} \\ \boldsymbol{B} & \boldsymbol{D} \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \\ \boldsymbol{\kappa} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad (\boldsymbol{A}, \boldsymbol{B}, \boldsymbol{D}) = \int_{-h/2}^{h/2} \bar{\boldsymbol{Q}}(z) (1, z, z^2) \, dz\end{split}\]

with

(2.2)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{A} = \begin{bmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} & A_{16} \\ A_{12} & A_{22} & A_{26} \\ A_{16} & A_{26} & A_{66} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

and likewise \(\boldsymbol{B}\) and \(\boldsymbol{D}\). \(\boldsymbol{A}\) is the membrane stiffness (force per unit length), \(\boldsymbol{B}\) the membrane-bending coupling (force) and \(\boldsymbol{D}\) the bending stiffness (force times length). \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\) is the total membrane strain of Eq. (1.10), linear plus nonlinear, such that \(\boldsymbol{N}\) and \(\boldsymbol{M}\) are the resultants of the current configuration in a nonlinear analysis.

The integral over \(z\) is the one of a flat laminate: the factor \(1 + z/R\) of the area of a layer of the curved wall is neglected, consistently with the shallow-shell thickness integration of the kinematics. The stiffnesses have no terms in \(h/R\), which limits the element to thin walls. \(z\) is positive outwards, as \(w\), such that a ply at the bottom of the laminate, the smallest \(z\), is at the inner surface of the cylinder. The stiffnesses are referred to the mid-surface of the element: a laminate whose reference surface is offset from its mid-plane has nonzero \(\boldsymbol{B}\) terms from the offset, which the stiffness matrices take into account, while the mass matrix of Eq. (4.10) assumes the reference surface at the middle of the thickness. The transverse shear stiffnesses of a laminate are not used: the Kirchhoff-Love kinematics have no transverse shear strain.

2.2. Stiffnesses at the integration points#

The element stores the 18 entries A11, …, D66 as arrays of shape (nint, nint), one value per integration point, the entry [i, j] at \(\xi\) = points[i] and \(\eta\) = points[j], see The BFSC element on the cylinder. Every integral of the element is evaluated with the \(\boldsymbol{A}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}\) and \(\boldsymbol{D}\) of the integration point, such that:

  • a laminate uniform over the element has the same values at every point, which bfsccylinder.utils.assign_constant_ABD() copies from an object with the attributes A11, …, D66, e.g. a laminate of composites.laminated_plate() or composites.isotropic_plate();

  • a laminate whose stiffness varies over the shell, e.g. a variable-angle laminate whose fiber angles vary along \(x\), is described point by point, by setting the arrays to the stiffnesses of the laminate at the position of each integration point.

The fiber angles of the laminate are measured in the plane \(x y\) of the shell, \(x\) axial and \(y\) circumferential. The stiffnesses are integrated exactly only when they are constant over the element, see Numerical integration; with a varying stiffness, the integration points sample it, and the error decreases as the mesh is refined.

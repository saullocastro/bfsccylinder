4. Element matrices and internal forces#

Every matrix and vector of the element follows from two energies, the strain energy of the kinematics of Kinematics of the cylindrical shell with the constitutive relation of Constitutive relation, and the kinetic energy, discretised with the interpolation of The BFSC element on the cylinder. The functions named below are those of bfsccylinder.bfsccylinder; bfsccylinder.bfsccylinder_sanders has the same functions, with the operators of the Sanders-type kinematics.

4.1. Strain energy#

With the membrane strains \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_L + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{NL}\) and the curvatures \(\boldsymbol{\kappa}\), the strain energy of the element is

(4.1)#\[U(\boldsymbol{u}_e) = \frac{1}{2} \int_A \left( \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} + 2 \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\top \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{\kappa} + \boldsymbol{\kappa}^\top \boldsymbol{D} \boldsymbol{\kappa} \right) dA\]

with, from Eqs. (3.6) to (3.9),

(4.2)#\[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \left( \boldsymbol{B}_m + \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \right) \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad \delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \left( \boldsymbol{B}_m + \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \right) \delta \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad \boldsymbol{\kappa} = \boldsymbol{B}_b \boldsymbol{u}_e\]

The factor \(1/2\) in the strains and its absence in their variation come from \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{NL}\) being quadratic in the displacements.

4.2. Internal force vector#

The internal force vector of update_fint() is the gradient of the strain energy:

(4.3)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{F}_{int} = \frac{\partial U}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e} = \int_A \left( \left( \boldsymbol{B}_m + \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \right)^\top \boldsymbol{N} + \boldsymbol{B}_b^\top \boldsymbol{M} \right) dA, \qquad \begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{N} \\ \boldsymbol{M} \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{A} & \boldsymbol{B} \\ \boldsymbol{B} & \boldsymbol{D} \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \\ \boldsymbol{\kappa} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

with the resultants of the current displacements. At equilibrium, \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\) balances the external forces, see Analyses.

4.3. Tangent stiffness matrix#

The Hessian of the strain energy, the Jacobian of \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\), is split into four matrices:

(4.4)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_T = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{F}_{int}}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e} = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} (\boldsymbol{u}_e) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{u}_e) + \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u}_e)\]

computed by update_KC0() (\(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\)), update_KCNL() (\(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\)) and update_KG() (\(\boldsymbol{K}_G\)). Since the three functions differentiate the same quadrature sums as update_fint(), \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) is the exact Jacobian of the discrete internal forces for any number of integration points, such that the Newton-Raphson iterations of Analyses converge quadratically.

Linear constitutive stiffness matrix. The term of the linear strains,

(4.5)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} = \int_A \left( \boldsymbol{B}_m^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_m + \boldsymbol{B}_m^\top \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{B}_b + \boldsymbol{B}_b^\top \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{B}_m + \boldsymbol{B}_b^\top \boldsymbol{D} \boldsymbol{B}_b \right) dA\]

does not depend on the displacements: it is the stiffness matrix of a linear static analysis and the tangent stiffness matrix at \(\boldsymbol{u}_e = \boldsymbol{0}\). It is symmetric. With the Sanders-type kinematics, \(\boldsymbol{B}_b\) has terms of \(u\) and \(v\), such that the bending stiffness \(\boldsymbol{D}\) couples the in-plane and the normal displacements even for a homogeneous wall, \(\boldsymbol{B} = \boldsymbol{0}\).

Nonlinear constitutive stiffness matrix. The terms of \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) in the derivative of \((\boldsymbol{B}_m + \boldsymbol{B}_{NL})^\top \boldsymbol{N}\) and of \(\boldsymbol{B}_b^\top \boldsymbol{M}\) with \(\boldsymbol{N}\) and \(\boldsymbol{M}\) varying are

(4.6)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} = \underbrace{\int_A \left( \boldsymbol{B}_m^\top \boldsymbol{A} + \boldsymbol{B}_b^\top \boldsymbol{B} \right) \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} dA}_{\boldsymbol{K}_{C0L}} + \underbrace{\int_A \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}^\top \left( \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_m + \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{B}_b \right) dA}_{\boldsymbol{K}_{CL0}} + \underbrace{\int_A \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} dA}_{\boldsymbol{K}_{CLL}}\]

\(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0L}\) and \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CL0}\) are linear in the displacements and \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CLL}\) is quadratic.

Geometric stiffness matrices. The derivative of \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) in \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}^\top \boldsymbol{N}\), with \(\boldsymbol{N}\) held fixed, gives a geometric stiffness matrix of the membrane resultants. The resultants are split into the part of the linear strains and the part of the nonlinear strains,

(4.7)#\[\boldsymbol{N}_L = \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_m \boldsymbol{u}_e + \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{B}_b \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad \boldsymbol{N}_{NL} = \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{NL}\]

and so is the geometric stiffness matrix:

(4.8)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{K}_G = \int_A \boldsymbol{G}^\top \begin{bmatrix} N_{Lxx} & N_{Lxy} \\ N_{Lxy} & N_{Lyy} \end{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{G} \, dA, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL} = \int_A \boldsymbol{G}^\top \begin{bmatrix} N_{NLxx} & N_{NLxy} \\ N_{NLxy} & N_{NLyy} \end{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{G} \, dA\end{split}\]

with the rows \(\boldsymbol{G}\) of the rotations, Eq. (3.8). Since \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}^\top \boldsymbol{N}\) is, row by row, \(\boldsymbol{G}^\top\) times the resultants times the slopes, its derivative at fixed \(\boldsymbol{N}\) is this quadratic form. update_KG() computes \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) and update_KCNL() adds \(\boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\) to \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\). The split keeps \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) homogeneous of degree one in the displacements, \(\boldsymbol{K}_G(\lambda \boldsymbol{u}_0) = \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u}_0)\), which is what a linear buckling analysis needs, while \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\) vanishes at zero displacements and collects the rest of the Hessian.

The geometric stiffness matrix depends on the kinematics through \(\boldsymbol{G}\). For the Donnell-type kinematics, \(\boldsymbol{G}\) has entries only at the degrees of freedom of \(w\), and \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) is the geometric stiffness matrix of a flat plate: the curvature of the shell enters a buckling problem only through \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) and the resultants. For the Sanders-type kinematics, the row \(\boldsymbol{S}^w_{,y} - \boldsymbol{S}^v/R\) populates the blocks \(v\)-\(v\) and \(v\)-\(w\), multiplied by \(N_{yy}\) and \(N_{xy}\) only; for a membrane state of axial compression, \(N_{yy} = N_{xy} = 0\), the two matrices coincide.

Geometric stiffness matrix of a constant stress state. update_KG_constant_stress() evaluates the \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) of Eq. (4.8) with given resultants \(N_{xx}\), \(N_{yy}\) and \(N_{xy}\), constant over the element, instead of those of a displacement field. It is linear in the given resultants, and is the geometric stiffness matrix of an assumed membrane state, e.g. a uniform axial compression without the bending boundary layers that the supports of a real cylinder produce.

4.4. Mass matrix#

The kinetic energy of the displacement field of Eq. (1.3), for a density \(\rho\) = rho uniform over the thickness \(h\) = h, with the shallow-shell volume \(dV = dz\, dA\) and the reference surface at the middle of the thickness, is

(4.9)#\[T = \frac{1}{2} \int_A \left( \rho h \left( \dot{u}^2 + \dot{v}^2 + \dot{w}^2 \right) + \rho \frac{h^3}{12} \left( \dot{\beta}_x^2 + \dot{\beta}_y^2 \right) \right) dA\]

the terms of \(\int z \, dz = 0\) vanishing. With Eqs. (3.5) and (3.8), the consistent mass matrix of update_M() is

(4.10)#\[\boldsymbol{M} = \int_A \rho \left( h \left( (\boldsymbol{S}^u)^\top \boldsymbol{S}^u + (\boldsymbol{S}^v)^\top \boldsymbol{S}^v + (\boldsymbol{S}^w)^\top \boldsymbol{S}^w \right) + \frac{h^3}{12} \boldsymbol{G}^\top \boldsymbol{G} \right) dA\]

The terms in \(h^3/12\) are the rotary inertia of the rotations of the normal of each kinematics: the Sanders-type rotation \(\beta_y = -w_{,y} + v/R\) couples \(v\) and \(w\) and adds \(\rho h^3/(12 R^2) (\boldsymbol{S}^v)^\top \boldsymbol{S}^v\), terms that the Donnell-type matrix does not have. The rotation used in the kinetic energy is the one of the strains, such that the rigid translation of Eq. (1.11) has the exact kinetic energy with the Sanders-type kinematics. The integrand is a polynomial and the matrix is computed in closed form from lex, ley, h, rho and, for the Sanders-type kinematics, R.
